    On 26 August 2026 a rock-ice slab fell from Langtang Lirung and became a debris flood that ran down the Bhote Koshi and Trishuli. The first SMS warning went out 38 minutes after the collapse. By then the flood had already passed Rasuwagadhi, Syabrubesi and the hydropower tunnels below them. This fictional protocol works out what could be decided, funded and pre-agreed before the next flash flood, on every timescale where a warning is physically possible, and is open about where a forecast-based protocol cannot help.

    §02 · RISK ANALYSIS

    Three ways a valley floods in minutes

    Nepal's deadliest floods do not only come down the big Terai rivers. In the hills and mountains they arrive as debris-laden surges from intense rain, failing glacial lakes, collapsing ice and rock, or landslide dams. Each has its own warning physics and its own season.

    Height above the river channel is a screening proxy for flood depth, not an inundation model. The 2026 debris flood splashed up to 800 m above the valley floor near its source, and 10–20 m in the middle reaches.

    Priority impacts

    The impacts this protocol acts on are ranked by harm and by whether anything done in the available lead time can reduce them. Loss of life dominates in every cascade. In 2026 it fell hardest on groups that household-based targeting misses: hydropower and road workers in tunnels and camps, traders and drivers at the border, and trekkers.

    §03 · TRIGGER METHODOLOGY

    A ladder, not a threshold

    No single forecast covers hazards whose warning time ranges from weeks (a dammed lake) to minutes (a collapsing slope). Each rung releases only the actions that fit its lead time and its false-alarm rate, and only rung 3b draws on the DREF.

        §05 · LEAD TIME AND THE LINKED WARNING SYSTEM

        Minutes are bought upstream

        For cascades there is no forecast. The lead time is the river's travel time from a sensor to a community, minus the time it takes to confirm the signal and send the alert. Move the sensor and change the delays to see who gets enough warning to reach high ground.

        Hydropower sites: warning time with the current explorer settings

        sites mapped in OpenStreetMap

        The linked warning system: who owns each piece today

        government / partner-owned · not an NRCS commitment until signed

        What the explorer shows

        • Detection is the binding constraint. On 26 August the alert came from a phone call at about 09:00. Even a perfect 3-minute chain triggered at the Betrawati gauge would have saved nobody above it. Sensors have to sit where the cascade starts: a seismic detector for mass movements, lake-level and camera stations, and gauges that alarm on how fast the water rises, not on its height.
        • Redundancy matters more than precision. The Rasuwagadhi, Syabrubesi and Betrawati gauges were destroyed before they reached their thresholds. A gauge that goes silent while the one upstream is rising is a signal in its own right.
        • Without hardware, people still help. Paid river watchers with radios upstream of side valleys, a siren code everybody knows, and phone trees are what NRCS can fund now (EA26). The 2026 school evacuation in Trishuli came from informal calls, not from the SMS.

        §07 · INTERVENTION AREA, TARGETING AND CASH

        Where, for whom, and how the money arrives

        The rain trigger watches all hill and mountain districts, but cash can only reach households that are registered in advance. Pre-registration is concentrated in the corridor palikas, where cascades and dammed lakes occur and the flood path is mapped.

        Targeting criteria

        1. Location: households less than 25 m above the river channel and within 3 km of it in a corridor palika (table), or in a ward with a recorded flash-flood death since 2011.
        2. Base list: the government's Social Security Allowance registers (senior citizens, single women, disability, endangered ethnic groups, child grants), as the SRSP standard requires, cross-checked with the palika's own list.
        3. Vulnerability within the flood path: landless and riverside-tenure households, households headed by women, persons with disabilities and older people. Ethnicity is not used as a marker: in Rasuwa or Sindhupalchok, Tamang and other Janajati households are the majority.
        4. Non-household groups: hydropower and road workers, border traders and drivers, trekkers and lodge staff. They are reached through operators, IPPAN, the tourism associations and gate rosters, not household registers.

        Lists are validated by the Local Disaster Management Committee and the ward, with a complaints desk and a free phone line before the first payment.

        §08 · IMPLEMENTATION, AUTHORITY AND READINESS

        Who decides what, and who does it

        NRCS activates its own actions and funds. It does not order evacuations, close roads or task the security forces. Under the DRRM Act 2074 those belong to the district and local disaster management committees, so the protocol depends on standing decisions those committees adopt before the season.
