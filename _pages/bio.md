He trains deep neural networks on data of the Earth system, with two applications in mind. The first is early warning of climate risks: within the EarthNet initiative, he works on forecasting drought impacts on vegetation from satellite imagery, with applications to anticipatory humanitarian action. The second is the carbon cycle, where he uses neural networks for atmospheric transport and inverse modeling of CO<sub>2</sub>. His group contributes to WeatherGenerator and EarthGenerator, two European projects building generative foundation models of the Earth system. Beyond his own research, he co-chairs the Risk KAN working group on AI for Complex Climate Risk Mitigation, co-founded the AI4Carbon community initiative, and has helped bring AI researchers and humanitarian practitioners together at the Global Dialogue Platform on Anticipatory Humanitarian Action. He has spoken about AI for early warning at the AI for Good Global Summit and at TEDx. He did his PhD in the ELLIS program at the Max Planck Institute for Biogeochemistry and ETH Zürich, after studying mathematics, mathematical physics and business in Göttingen, Barcelona, Leipzig and Stockholm.</p>
